## Early voting is how we won in 2024

For years Republicans saved their vote for Election Day. In 2024 the RNC finally stopped fighting early voting and started using it. Their Bank Your Vote push had one line that said it all: "When Republicans vote early, we win." ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/rnc-launches-bank-your-vote-ad-blitz-ahead-debate-push-republicans-vote-early-2024-elections))

Then look at what happened.

**North Carolina.** At the end of early voting in 2022, registered Democrats had cast about 155,000 more early ballots than registered Republicans. In 2024, registered Republicans finished early voting about 35,000 ballots *ahead* of Democrats. That's a swing of roughly 190,000 ballots in two years, straight from the [NC State Board of Elections](https://dl.ncsbe.gov/?prefix=ENRS/) ballot files. Trump carried North Carolina.

**Nevada.** By the final days of early voting, registered Republicans had cast 393,811 early ballots to 344,539 for Democrats, according to the Nevada Secretary of State ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/republicans-topple-democrat-voter-registration-advantage-in-crucial-swing-state-as-early-vote-wraps-up)). Trump won Nevada, the first Republican to do it since 2004 ([PBS NewsHour / AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/ap-race-call-donald-trump-wins-nevada)).

**Nationally**, more than 80 million Americans voted before Election Day, and NPR called Republicans voting early in person "one of the more surprising early voting patterns this year" ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/11/05/g-s1-32395/more-than-80-million-americans-cast-ballots-before-election-day)).

Party registration tells you who voted, not how they voted. But when we banked our votes early, we stopped spotting Democrats a three-week head start, and our campaigns could spend the final days chasing the voters who hadn't shown up yet instead of everyone.

## Where we are right now

As of the October 14 pull, in the 22 states that track party, registered Democrats have cast 53 of every 100 early ballots and registered Republicans have cast 28. Some of that is normal this early, because mail ballots come in first and in-person early voting hasn't opened in most states yet. But a midterm is won by whoever shows up, and we can't let that lead harden.

So here's the ask. If you don't trust mail ballots, fine. Vote early in person. Then text five people you know and ask when they're voting. We'll swamp Election Day, but we can't afford to leave a single vote on the table.
