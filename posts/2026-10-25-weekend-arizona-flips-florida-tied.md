Let's be honest. Weekends are slow for early vote data. A lot of states didn't post anything new today. But the ones that did tell the same story they've been telling all week, and two of them crossed a line worth talking about.

## The national lead keeps shrinking

The UF Election Lab tracks every state that registers voters by party. These are hard counts, not a model.

- **Thursday, October 22:** Democrats ahead by 17.0 points on 9.3 million ballots
- **Sunday, October 25:** Democrats ahead by 14.4 points on 13.7 million ballots

That's 4.4 million new ballots, and every batch came in less Democratic than the lead they built on early mail. Over 27 million Americans have voted early so far.

## Arizona flipped

On Thursday, Democrats led Arizona's early vote by 0.8 points. As of the latest count, **Republicans lead by 0.3**, 38.7% to 38.4% on about 861,000 ballots.

Is 0.3 points a landslide? Come on. No. But Arizona is a state Democrats were counting on, and the direction is the story. Every day this week it moved our way.

## Florida is a coin toss

Florida registers by party, so this is another hard count. On Thursday, Democrats led the early vote by 3.3 points. Today it's **D +0.2**, 39.8% to 39.7%, on 2.44 million ballots.

That gap went from about 55,000 ballots to about 2,500 in three days. Florida Republicans historically vote in person and late, which is exactly what's happening. Expect it to flip in the next pull or two.

## Texas: 3.6 million and counting

Texans have cast **3,573,554** early ballots. That's already about 65% of the entire 2022 early vote, with a full week of early voting left.

Election Data Guy's estimate has Republicans at 55.3% and Democrats at 43.3%, about **R +12**. That number has barely moved since Thursday. Texas doesn't register voters by party, so it's an estimate, not a state count. But a steady R +12 on 3.6 million ballots is not what "Texas is in play" looks like. If you missed it, I broke down [why primary voter math is misleading people](/blog/texas-primary-voter-math/).

## North Carolina still worries me

North Carolina posts every ballot by return date, so I can line it up against past years on the same day before the election. Ten days out:

- **2022:** Democrats ahead by 8.1
- **2024:** Republicans ahead by 1.1, and Trump won the state
- **2026:** Democrats ahead by 17.6

It's better than it was. A week ago that gap was over 25 points. But North Carolina Republicans are still well behind where they were in 2022, a year they won the Senate race. If you're in North Carolina, go this week.

## Where the swing states stand

States with weekend updates are marked with the latest pull. The rest are as of Saturday.

- **Michigan:** D +0.3 (estimate)
- **Arizona:** R +0.3
- **Georgia:** D +2.4 (estimate)
- **Wisconsin:** D +2.9 (estimate)
- **Nevada:** D +3.8
- **North Carolina:** D +17.5 (registered Democrats vs. Republicans, with a big unaffiliated share)

Michigan, Georgia and Wisconsin don't register by party either, so those are estimates too.
